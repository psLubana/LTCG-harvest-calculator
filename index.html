Before you place the orders

Oldest shares go first. For shares held in demat, the oldest purchase is treated as sold first (FIFO). You can't pick a cheaper or newer lot of the same stock, so this calculator doesn't either. Add buy dates so the order is right.

Buy back the next trading day. Selling and rebuying the same stock on the same day can be netted by your broker and show up as an intraday trade instead of a long-term sale.

Refresh prices just before selling. The plan is only as good as the price you typed. Market orders can fill above or below it, which is what the safety buffer is for.

Pre-2018 shares use the grandfathering rule. For shares bought on or before 31 January 2018, the cost is the higher of what you paid and the lower of the 31 January 2018 closing price and today's price. Your broker's tax P&L usually lists that price.

Charges. Brokerage on the sale reduces your gain, but securities transaction tax (STT) does not. The calculator ignores both, which keeps it slightly on the safe side.

Bonus shares, splits and mergers change your cost and dates. Use the figures from your broker's statement for those lots; bonus shares usually have a cost of ₹0.

This is a planning tool, not tax advice. It assumes you are a resident individual and that every lot is listed equity or an equity-oriented fund on which STT was paid. The final figure is the one in your tax return, which also considers things like surcharge and set-off order. Check with a chartered accountant for unusual cases.
